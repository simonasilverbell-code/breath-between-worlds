# Research questions

The public showcase is intentionally organized around questions that can be tested rather than around claims that are difficult to falsify.

## 1. Biography-conditioned choice

Can a later resident decision be causally connected to a specific earlier personal experience rather than merely regenerated from current context?

A convincing test should compare a resident with its intact autobiographical trace against a controlled counterfactual where the relevant memory is absent or altered while the rest of the state is held as constant as possible.

## 2. Acquired skill

Can a resident improve a practical behavior because of its own previous failed or successful attempts?

The baseline checkpoint contains different construction outcomes, but that alone is not evidence of learning. Future tests should trace which earlier observations are retrieved, whether method parameters change, and whether performance improves across repeated trials.

## 3. Spatial causality

How does behavior change when the world stops acting like a mostly flat grid and begins to model visibility, occlusion, elevation, enclosure, depth, traversability and sound propagation?

The goal is richer causality, not cosmetic 3D.

## 4. Social provenance

When one resident learns something from another, can the system preserve who observed it first, who transmitted it, and how confidence changes across the chain?

## 5. Emergence versus menu effects

Which apparently surprising behaviors are genuinely produced by interaction between memory, environment and decision policy, and which are artifacts of the available action menu?

The project will keep these categories separate in future reports.
